# Ping Pong Parachute

## Summary

I designed and tested a bottle-rocket and parachute system with the goal of keeping a ping pong ball airborne for as long as possible. I optimized the rocket for launch height and stability while separately testing parachute materials, size, suspension lines, and deployment before combining the two systems. The final design placed **3rd nationally in 2022**.

### Key Results

- **3rd nationally — 2022**
- Final parachute descent rate of roughly **0.898 ft/s**
- Tested the rocket and parachute separately to isolate variables before full-system testing
- Optimized rocket mass, stability, parachute material, canopy size, and suspension-line configuration
- Final competition parachute used lightweight painter's plastic with a diameter of roughly **85 cm**

- I selected a lightweight, relatively aerodynamic bottle and tried to remove unnecessary mass so more of the stored pressure could go toward launch height.
- I used an extremely lightweight plastic tube for the upper structure since this was one of the largest sources of unnecessary weight.
- I tested fin configurations to balance stability and drag. Three fins could reduce weight and drag, while four gave the rocket more stability.
- Keeping the rocket straight and balanced was extremely important because even small alignment errors could cause a curved flight and hurt parachute deployment.

- I experimented with different lightweight materials, including painter's plastic and Mylar, to maximize parachute area while keeping mass as low as possible.
- I tested canopy diameter, suspension-line length, number of lines, and how the lines connected to the ping pong ball.
- Larger parachutes produced more drag, but they were also harder to deploy consistently and required more suspension lines to fully open.
- The final competition design used thin painter's plastic with a diameter of roughly **85 cm**.

- I tested the **parachute separately from the rocket** whenever possible so I could compare parachute designs much faster without wasting full launches.
- I also tested the rocket with lower-value parachutes first so I could work on launch consistency without damaging the best parachute designs.
- Deployment became just as important as the parachute itself. Tangled suspension lines, a crooked launch, or strings catching on the rocket could remove several seconds from the flight.
- I kept changing one major variable at a time and then combined the best rocket and parachute configurations for full-system testing.

**Parachute Test → Rocket Test → Combine Systems → Observe → Adjust → Repeat**

<br>

## Outcome & Takeaways

- This project taught me how useful it is to break a larger system into smaller parts and optimize them separately before testing everything together.
- It also showed me how much small details matter in a lightweight design. A little extra mass, a tangled line, or a slightly crooked rocket could completely change the final result.
